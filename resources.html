Top 8 SQA Exam Pitfalls in Organic Reactions (And How to Score Full Marks)

1. Reversing Ester Names from Structural Formulae

Trap: Examiners frequently draw esters "backwards" with the alcohol part on the left: CH₃CH₂–O–C(=O)CH₂CH₂CH₃.
Fix: Always locate the C=O carbonyl carbon first! The chain attached to C=O is the -oate (butanoate), and the chain attached to the single-bonded –O– is the -yl (ethyl) → ethyl butanoate.

2. Confusing Protein Denaturation with Hydrolysis

Trap: Writing that heating an enzyme "breaks peptide links".
Fix: Heating or changing pH causes denaturation, which breaks intermolecular hydrogen bonds holding the 3D folded shape. Breaking covalent peptide (amide) links (–CONH–) requires hydrolysis (+H₂O with acid reflux or digestive enzymes).

3. Incomplete Explanations of Fat vs Oil Melting Points

Trap: Saying "oils have double bonds so they have weaker bonds to break".
Fix: Never imply covalent C=C bonds break during melting! State clearly: 1) Oils are more unsaturated (C=C); 2) C=C causes bends/kinks that prevent chains packing closely; 3) Therefore London dispersion forces (LDFs) between molecules are weaker.

4. Trivalent or Pentavalent Carbon Atoms in Drawings

Trap: Leaving out a hydrogen atom on a carbon chain or drawing 5 bonds around a carbonyl carbon.
Fix: Before moving to the next question, count the covalent lines around every carbon atom: every carbon must have exactly 4 bonds. Also ensure the bond to an –OH group goes to the O atom (C–O–H), not the H atom!

5. Misstating Oxidising Agent Colour Changes

Trap: Writing "clear" instead of "colourless" for Tollens' or bromine, or mixing up Fehling's and dichromate.
Fix: Always use the word colourless (never "clear"). Memorise: Dichromate = Orange → Green; Hot CuO = Black → Brown/Pink; Fehling's = Blue → Brick-red ppt; Tollens' = Colourless → Silver mirror.

6. Forgetting the 1 : 3 Stoichiometry in Fats & Oils

Trap: Assuming 1 mole of fat produces 1 mole of fatty acid or 3 moles of glycerol.
Fix: Remember the prefix tri- in triglyceride/triester: 1 mole of glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) + 3 moles of fatty acid ⇌ 1 mole of fat/oil + 3 moles of H₂O.

7. Mixing Up Soap Heads vs Food Emulsifier Heads

Trap: Saying food emulsifiers have an ionic carboxylate head.
Fix: Soaps have an ionic carboxylate head (–COO⁻Na⁺). Food emulsifiers (mono-/diglycerides) have polar unreacted hydroxyl (–OH) groups on the glycerol backbone as their hydrophilic head.

8. Getting Antioxidant Redox Role Backwards

Trap: Saying antioxidants are oxidising agents.
Fix: Antioxidants (like Vitamin C) prevent food oxidation by undergoing oxidation themselves (losing electrons: 2e⁻ on the right of the ion-electron equation). Because they are oxidised, they act as reducing agents.
