Open-Ended Q1: Identifying C₃H₆O Isomers 3 Marks

"A student finds three unlabelled bottles in a chemistry prep room. Each bottle contains a colourless liquid with the molecular formula C₃H₆O. The three liquids are known to be propanal, propanone, and prop-2-en-1-ol (CH₂=CHCH₂OH). Using your knowledge of chemistry, comment on how the student could positively identify all three liquids."

SQA 3-Mark Model Answer Rubric:
1. Functional Group Identification: Propanal has an aldehyde (–CHO) group; propanone has a ketone (–CO–) group; prop-2-en-1-ol has both an alkene (C=C) double bond and a primary alcohol (–OH) group.
2. Test 1 (Bromine Solution): Shake a sample of each with orange/brown bromine solution (Br₂(aq)). Only prop-2-en-1-ol contains a C=C double bond and will rapidly decolourise the bromine solution via an addition reaction.
3. Test 2 (Tollens’ or Fehling’s): Warm the remaining two liquids in a hot water bath with Tollens’ reagent (or Fehling’s solution). Propanal (the aldehyde) is oxidised to propanoic acid and forms a silver mirror (or a brick-red precipitate with Fehling’s), whereas propanone (the ketone) resists oxidation and gives no change.
Open-Ended Q2: Fats, Oils, Soaps & Detergents 3 Marks

"Butter, olive oil, and dishwashing liquid all interact differently with water and heat. Using your knowledge of chemistry, explain the relationship between the chemical structures of fats, oils, and soaps/detergents and their physical properties and everyday uses."

SQA 3-Mark Model Answer Rubric:
1. Triester Structure & Melting Point: Butter (fat) and olive oil (oil) are both triesters (triglycerides) formed from glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) and three fatty acids. Olive oil has a higher degree of unsaturation (C=C double bonds), causing bends/kinks in the chains that prevent close packing, resulting in weaker London dispersion forces and a lower melting point (liquid at room temp) than saturated butter fat.
2. Soap & Detergent Structure: Alkaline hydrolysis of fats/oils with NaOH produces soap. Soaps and dishwashing detergents have a long non-polar hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail (dissolves in non-polar grease/oil) and an ionic hydrophilic head (dissolves in polar water).
3. Micelles & Hard Water: Agitation forms negatively charged spherical micelles that repel each other, suspending grease in water as an emulsion. Dishwashing liquid uses soapless detergents so it does not form insoluble scum with Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ ions in hard water.
